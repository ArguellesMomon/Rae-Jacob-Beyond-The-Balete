# Folklore guides in Turning Paths

The optional maze guides are now a Tigmamanukan and Aling Dalisay, an original giantess. Nilo and Sela remain duwende in Lantern Clearing; older village conversations do not become encounters with the new creatures.

## Encounters

- The blue bird perches near the beginning of each trial. It opens its beak, blinks, and periodically flaps a short way toward the next real corridor before returning. Inspecting it gives Rae a curious first meeting and Tala an opportunity to discuss travel omens. The bird never speaks human dialogue. Repeat inspections give the seeded direction immediately.
- Dalisay rests farther along the solution route in a small side clearing. Her seated silhouette is larger than Rae, with round ears, silver-threaded hair, woven clothing, warm eyes, subtle breathing, blinking, speaking mouth and four pointing poses. She gives the next direction without adding an errand or prerequisite. Repeat conversations are shorter.
- Worn arrows, petals and leaf ties remain sparse. Each trial still requires only its one destination sign. Directions derive from that trial’s actual graph.
- The clearing is part of the shared painted ground and collision surface. It fits between neighboring grid roads without joining disconnected corridors. NPC footings leave every route center open. Actors use their own dimensions and foot anchors and sort by ground depth.
- Still mode freezes both creatures. New portraits appear in dialogue, People and the Codex.

## Recorded traditions and our fiction

**Tigmamanukan:** Plasencia’s 1589 *Customs of the Tagalogs*, in Blair and Robertson vol. VII, printed p. 189, records a bird named Tigmamanuguin and travel decisions based on favorable/unfavorable calls. This is an early colonial account of a Tagalog practice. Our blue bird’s repeated flights toward a destination and friendship with Rae are game inventions. No universal compass rule or ability to rescue every lost person is claimed.

[Historical account](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4c/The_Philippine_Islands%2C_1493-1803%3B_%28IA_afk2830.0001.007.umich.edu%29.pdf)

**Dalisay:** Morice Vanoverbergh’s *Isneg Tales*, “The Giant’s Wife,” printed p. 64 (PDF page 64), records Sibbarayungan sheltering Ayo from Gisurab, sharing food, and Ayo returning home. The recorded episode supports protective hospitality, rather than magical route-finding. Dalisay is an original character inspired by that episode, not the traditional figure renamed. Her name, costume, giantess resting place and directions are fictional. Her plain woven clothes do not reproduce sacred regalia. The Codex does not describe all Isneg giants as benevolent.

[Isneg Tales](https://asianethnology.org/article/148006-isneg-tales.pdf)

## Persistence and review

`chapterTwo.helperChats` stores completed encounters separately from resident visits. Old saves gain empty encounter records without changing maze seeds, earned trials or village memories. Only completed encounters unlock each new Codex and People entry. Replay still uses its separate save slot.

Verification covers randomized directions, NPC approach/targeting, all corridor centers, clearing boundaries, optional help, repeat dialogue, real controller movement and save/load, legacy/corrupt encounter fields, four poses, rasterized portraits, visible flight frames and Still mode. Source-art scene reviews are generated by `scripts/review-maze-wayfinding.ts` under `artifacts/folklore-guides/`.
