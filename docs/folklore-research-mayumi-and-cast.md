# Folklore research — Mayumi and the existing cast

Research date: **2026-10-18**. Purpose: ground the game in identifiable Philippine traditions and support cultural and language literacy. This is a working research basis, not a claim to exhaust the oral traditions of the Philippines. No new character or map is implemented by this research pass.

## Mayumi's identity and the evidence

Keep the user-confirmed **Mayumi, The Diwata ng Bukal**. None of the reliable accounts reviewed establishes Mayumi as the inherited personal name of a traditional spring deity. That finding is limited to the sources checked; it cannot rule out every local oral account. Her personal history, appearance, relationships and involvement with Silong's seals remain original fiction.

## Diwata does not have one identical meaning everywhere

The NCCA's [Hiligaynon literature account](https://ncca.gov.ph/about-ncca-3/subcommissions/subcommission-on-the-arts-sca/literary-arts/hiligaynon-literature/) discusses diwata in relation to spirits, ritual chants, health, home and agriculture. Its [Talaandig account](https://ncca.gov.ph/about-ncca-3/subcommissions/subcommission-on-cultural-communities-and-traditional-arts-sccta/central-cultural-communities/the-talaandig/) names particular diwata in household protection. These are different community contexts. Neither establishes the role or customs of every diwata.

The NCCA also describes [Mariang Makiling as a guardian of Mount Makiling](https://www.flickr.com/photos/nccaofficial/18410226086). Her association is with that named landscape. Mayumi's connection to a fictional spring should be equally specific within our story; it does not make her Mariang Makiling or give her ownership of every spring.

[Diksiyonaryo.ph's diwata entry](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/diwata) includes a female-deity sense and figurative uses involving beauty. A dictionary sense describes vocabulary; it does not replace the regional accounts above or supply a single complete mythology. Mayumi's characterization should develop her work, decisions and responsibilities as well as her appearance.

## Grounding the existing characters

| Character | Documented motif and source | Original Silong interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mang Uban / kapre | The [NCCA's kapre entry](https://www.flickr.com/photos/nccaofficial/18442068642) describes a tobacco-smoking giant associated with sitting in trees. | Mang Uban's name, silver beard, clothing, patience, friendships and Listening Seed. His intimidating silhouette can retain the documented scale and tobacco motif without making his personal behavior a rule for all kapre. |
| Bagtas / tikbalang | The [NCCA's tikbalang entry](https://www.flickr.com/photos/nccaofficial/18070087763) describes horse-like features, a humanlike body and hands, misleading travelers, and a sunshower/wedding saying. | Bagtas's physique, boastfulness, survey cord, shelter and landmark puzzle. The saying can be remembered as something Lola told Rae; it is not proof that Bagtas is getting married whenever it rains. |
| Tala / duwende | A [2015 recorded family account](https://folklore.usc.edu/duwende/) emphasizes small stature and mischief; a [2017 account](https://folklore.usc.edu/duwende-2/) emphasizes trees and consequences of disturbing their inhabitants. | Tala's name, mapmaking, clothing, leaf clasp, broad tapered ears and friendship with Rae. Her ears are an artistic identifier in this game, not a verified universal anatomical feature of duwende. The narrators' differing accounts should not be flattened into one rule. |

The USC entries are records of individual tellings, including diaspora family memories. Use the performers' accounts as evidence of those tellings, while distinguishing student commentary from what the narrator said. Claims about illness or spirits are recorded beliefs, not established medical explanations.

## Courtesy and belonging

A [Caloocan family account of tabi-tabi po](https://folklore.usc.edu/tabi-tabi-po/) connects the expression with respecting a mound and its possible inhabitants. It supports a particular family tradition, not a universal passphrase that controls every supernatural being.

**Writing decision:** Rae can remember Lola's advice and ask Mayumi before approaching the spring or filling a cup. Show courtesy through his actions: waiting, looking, listening and avoiding damage. An exact recitation should not function as a required magic password. This is our narrative application, not a documented Mayumi ritual.

## Language and literacy in the spring scene

Use short explanations tied to things the player can see. The following English glosses are paraphrases checked against Diksiyonaryo.ph:

| Word in this scene | Practical meaning | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bukal** | The source where water emerges. | [bukal](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/bukal) |
| **Batis** | A small stream, including one fed by a spring. | [batis](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/batis) |
| **Agos** | The direction or movement of flowing water. | [agos](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/agos) |

For example, Rae can first mistake the visible stream for the source. Tala points out where the water emerges; Mayumi explains why tending the source helps those farther downstream. That exchange and the puzzle are original writing. The language is learned through a concrete action, with an optional notebook explanation available afterward.

## Decisions for Mayumi's chapter

1. Introduce Mayumi doing the work of a spring caretaker. Make her relationship with the bukal visible before delivering an explanation of the world.
2. Let her be generous and firm, with priorities of her own. Rae's need to go home matters, while other inhabitants' needs also shape her choices.
3. Tie her powers and the immediate problem to this particular spring. The precise limits of her magic are authored Silong rules and must be explained in the story.
4. Give Rae a child's first reaction: astonishment, nervous questions and curiosity about what she can do. He gradually learns to distinguish a family story from the individual before him.
5. Let Tala contribute observations, and let Bagtas's confidence meet evidence. Mayumi is a participant in the investigation, not a character who answers every mystery on arrival.
6. Keep the Heart Balete, seals, crossing network and Listening Seed labeled as original worldbuilding. Neither of the water-spirit references establishes those mechanics.
7. If an optional folklore note names a real tradition, include its place/community and a source. A future notebook could distinguish **In the recorded story** from **In Silong**. This is a proposed presentation, not a feature implemented in this pass.

## Source handling and remaining limits

- Favor cultural institutions, attributed fieldwork, named scholarship and identifiable narrators. Search-result popularity does not establish traditional origin.
- Some institutional pages were available through indexed excerpts while direct retrieval failed. Those retrieval limits do not turn a short excerpt into a full reading of the linked work.
- Modern uploaded school stories featuring a character called Mayumi were found. They do not establish that our Mayumi has an ancient, shared biography. They are not the basis for her lore.
- Translated European fairy tales also appeared in searches using the Filipino word *diwata*. Translation into Filipino alone does not make those stories Philippine folklore.
- Do not infer mandatory wings, a universal costume, a fixed hierarchy, an exact ritual or a single moral alignment from the general word *diwata*.
- The reviewed evidence does not verify a historical garment or sacred design for Mayumi. Her planned water-toned clothing remains an original visual choice. Research specific regional clothing and symbolism separately before attributing them to a community.
- A published summary is a starting point. A direct retelling of a particular epic or a community's living ritual calls for fuller work with its versions, narrators and context before implementation.
